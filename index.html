On paying attention, building small,
and keeping a notebook

October 11, 2026   ·   By Akash

There are things you read and forget. And then there are things that stay with you: a sentence in a book, a tool that changes how you work, an idea you keep returning to.

This is a place for the second kind. A running collection of notes, questions, discoveries, and small experiments. Some finished thoughts. Plenty still in progress.

A place for the sentences that stay

A highlighted passage is a beginning. The interesting part comes afterward: why did I stop here? What does this connect to? Does it change anything about the way I see the world?

The note I want to keep is more than what the book said. It’s what happened in my head when I read it.

An original thought for this sample entry. Future posts can include book titles, selected highlights, and the reflections they sparked.

· · ·

From “that’s interesting” to “let me try”

There’s a difference between reading about a new AI tool and spending an afternoon with it. The useful questions are usually ordinary ones. What did I ask it to do? Where did it help? What did I still have to figure out myself?

That’s the kind of writing I want here: a little context, a real experiment, and an honest account of what I learned. A useful link is even better when there’s a reason attached to it.

Building a small corner of the internet

A blog feels like the right-sized home for all of this. One entry can hold a book note, an unfinished idea, a discovery, and something I’ve made. The headings can change each time. The only recurring theme is curiosity.

No elaborate system. Just a title, a date, and enough room to think out loud.

What makes a note worth returning to?

October 7, 2026   ·   By Akash

Saving something is easy. Finding a reason to return to it takes a little more attention.

This is sample writing to show how an older entry appears in the feed. Each post can contain its complete text here, with its own title, date, and whichever headings fit the writing.

Beyond the highlight

A notebook can hold more than collected sentences. It can hold questions: what surprised me, what I disagree with, and what I want to try after reading.

A short reflection beside a passage makes it easier to remember why it mattered. The useful part is the connection between someone else’s idea and a question of my own.

· · ·

A small experiment for the next few days

Choose one idea from a reading session and put it to use. Write a paragraph, make a small prototype, or explain it to someone. Then record what became clearer and what still feels unresolved.

Notebook / 003Sample older entry

A little less collecting, a little more trying

October 3, 2026   ·   By Akash

A list of interesting tools can grow forever. A small experiment gives that list a purpose.

This is another sample entry. Older posts stay open in the same reading stream, so there’s no need to click through to finish them.

The question before the tool

Start with something specific to do: organize a few notes, explore an unfamiliar topic, or build a simple page. A clear task makes it easier to notice what a tool actually contributes.

What belongs in the write-up

The goal, the attempt, the result, and the surprise. Those four things can turn an afternoon of experimenting into something useful to share.

Sometimes the result is a working project. Sometimes it’s a better question. Both can belong in the notebook.

Until the next entry.
— Akash
